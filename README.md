# Article Five Lab

An evidence-led interactive explainer of what public institutions and audited logistics evidence can—and cannot—tell us about a hypothetical Russian attack on NATO territory.

**[Open the interactive live website](https://article-five-lab.lindgreendavid.chatgpt.site)**

## Research question

What political, legal and logistical processes would public evidence support expecting after an armed attack on NATO territory, without inventing operational plans or predicting a war outcome?

## Core result

The best public model is not a single Article 5 countdown. It is two coupled systems:

1. classification, consent and Alliance coordination; and
2. physical movement and sustainment across civilian–military infrastructure.

Article 5 creates an obligation to assist, but it does not prescribe an automatic declaration of war or an identical military contribution from every Ally. EU funding and project counts demonstrate activity, not end-to-end reinforcement readiness.

## What the website includes

- an incident and attribution explorer;
- a five-stage Article 5 process map;
- an interactive European mobility bottleneck model;
- an accessible Europe evidence map with decision, mobility and civil-resilience layers;
- a five-phase animated response theatre spanning conventional, air/missile, cyber and hybrid incidents;
- a claim-audit tool for common misconceptions;
- an explicit evidence boundary and methods section;
- a primary-source register checked on 16 August 2026;
- a machine-readable evidence register in `data/evidence-register.json`.

## Scope and safety

This is an independent educational research product. It contains no classified material, operational routes, targeting information, tactical recommendations, probability of escalation, weapons-effect modelling or prediction of victory. It is not affiliated with NATO, the European Union or any government.

The supplied research brief and Google Drive source collection were used as lead registers. Displayed claims were independently checked against the linked official primary sources; the map is institutional context, not an operational route plan.

The response theatre visualizes public defensive functions using four distinct visual grammars: staged assistance, surveillance and protection, distributed cyber coordination, and pattern detection under uncertainty. Its rings, scans, packets and connecting lines are conceptual—not attack vectors, reinforcement routes, targets, probabilities or measured response times.
